How to read this map
Two sensor networks shown together. Circles = PurpleAir (low-cost optical, ~3 m above ground, 10-min samples, EPA Barkjohn-corrected (Barkjohn et al., AMT 2021)). Diamonds = regulatory AirNow / EPA FRM monitors (gold-standard hourly aggregated). Both show each site's maximum value during the fire window; color follows EPA's 2024 PM2.5 AQI band, size scales with magnitude.

Compare the two: regulatory monitors confirm the plume direction (closest downwind site, NJDEP's South Camden monitor at 0.4 mi N, rose to ~33 µg/m³ during the burn). Corrected PurpleAir peaks now align much closer to regulatory values; the residual gap reflects PurpleAir's 10-min cadence catching sub-hour spikes that hourly regulatory averages smooth.

Gray bands are the modeled smoke tracer footprint — locations where a unit-source NOAA HYSPLIT run (HRRR 3-km met, 30-min averaged column concentration 0–100 m AGL, union of 10/30/100/200 m release heights) predicted modeled tracer mass at any point in the window. The bands are relative, not measured or estimated µg/m³, and the model carries no calibration to a specific fire emission rate. Only the shape and direction of the modeled plume are meaningful; the cumulative footprint shows where, not when.

Mass remains airborne in the simulation (no dry or wet deposition). Sensor peaks and modeled footprint cores do not always overlap because the model averages a 0–100 m air column over 500 m cells, while sensors sample 10-minute PM at ~3 m above ground in light, shifting winds.
